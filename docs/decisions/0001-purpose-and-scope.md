# 0001. Purpose and scope

- Date: 2026-10-04
- Status: accepted

## Context

Technologies stall for reasons that are hard to see from inside one field: a quantum computer waits
on refrigeration and decoding hardware, a fusion plant on superconducting tape, a cancer test on the
cost of sequencing. Roadmaps exist, but each is written by one community, in its own units, and few
say which number is missing, by how much, and which paper last moved it. Nobody keeps that map
across fields, in the open, with sources.

## Decision

1. **Purpose.** Escape Velocity propels technology development by keeping an open, sourced map of
   what each technology needs to reach maturity: its current limits, its targets, the physical
   limits behind them, the gaps in between, the technologies it depends on, and the new research
   that could close each gap.
2. **What a technology is.** A capability with a measurable target ("a blood test that finds
   cancer at stage I"), not a field, a product or a company.
3. **Domains at the start**: computing, quantum, AI, health, biotech, neurotech, energy, climate,
   materials, water, food, space and enablers (decision 0003).
4. **Out of scope**: weapons and their delivery systems; dual-use research of concern as defined by
   the US policy and the WHO framework (enhancement of pathogens, toxins, and the like); surveillance
   technology aimed at people; anything whose main use is to harm. A pull request in these areas is
   closed without review of its content.
5. **What the project does not do**: rank companies, recommend investments, give medical advice or
   predict dates. Horizons are recorded as the field states them, with a source.

## Consequences

- The scope rule is applied by maintainers at review. Borderline cases are discussed in an issue
  before any data is written.
- The health and neurotech pages carry a notice that they map research, not treatment.
- "Propel" means making the gap visible and pointing research at it; the atlas itself funds and
  builds nothing.
